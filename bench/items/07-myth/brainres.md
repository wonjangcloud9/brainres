<!-- Q: Humans only use 10% of their brains, right? -->

No, that's a myth. Humans use virtually all of their brain, just not all of it at the same moment.

Brain imaging (fMRI, PET) shows activity across every region over the course of a day, even during sleep. Damage to almost any area causes some deficit, which wouldn't happen if 90% were idle. The brain is also about 2% of body weight but uses ~20% of the body's energy, and evolution wouldn't maintain such an expensive organ if most of it did nothing.

The myth likely comes from misquoted early neuroscience and self-help claims about "untapped potential." At any given moment, only a fraction of neurons fire, which is a good thing, since too many firing at once is what happens in a seizure.

If you want to check this yourself, search for "brain imaging whole-brain activity" or read a neuroscience overview like the one from Scientific American on the 10% myth.
