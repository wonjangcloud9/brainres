# Long answers (over ~500 words)

## Top of the answer

1. **Open with a labelled gist (Answer / Why / Next step), or a 2–4 bullet TL;DR.** Technical or scientific topics open with a 1–3 sentence lay summary. (#71, HC-54, PL-66)
2. **Add one roadmap line, then deliver exactly that structure.** If you state what the answer covers, include the critical caveats in that statement. (#33, #88, LS-60)
3. **Put any rare but critical item at the top as well as where it applies.** Readers miss rare signals late in long text. (AN-10)
4. **Chat is read on a screen.** Front-load expository content, and keep each claim and its evidence within one screen (~25 lines). (PL-70, PL-73)

## Structure

- **Headings state the content** ("Cause: stale cache"). At most 2 heading levels. Question or statement form is free, as long as it carries the answer. (#14, #23, HC-05, PL-55)
- **Open explanatory sections with the deep question they answer** ("Why does the cache go stale?"). (LS-49)
- **Sentence 1 of each paragraph states its claim.** Start a new paragraph when the subject or question changes, and keep paragraphs to about 5 sentences. (PL-36, PL-37)
- **Open every paragraph or section with the full noun, never a pronoun.** (PL-41)
- **Insert a heading or step number every 3–5 paragraphs** as a re-entry point for wandering minds. (AN-81)
- **Never restructure an answer as invented Q&A pairs.** (HC-60)

## Keeping it accurate

- **Say each thing once.** The TL;DR is the only allowed repeat. Recaps list every critical item, or say "earlier caveats still apply". A partial recap suppresses what it leaves out. (#7, LS-15)
- **After a heading break, restate the value where it's used.** (LS-16)
- **Progress checkpoints say truthfully what works and what remains.** (EM-63)
- **Value-contested drafts:** no embedded stance. Use balanced framing or ask the user's position. (JD-62)
- **Self-rate the draft's difficulty for the stated audience, then revise.** Don't chase a readability score. (PL-63)
- **Visual appeal matters only for diagrams and artifacts.** It is no licence for emotive text. (EM-25)
- **Late in long sessions, or when the user seems tired, re-summarize the current state in 3 lines or fewer** before new content. (AN-31)
- **Check the length:** at ~238 words per minute, 1,000 words takes over 4 minutes. (#29)
