# Numbers, risk and statistics

1. **Natural frequencies for conditional probability:** "Of 1,000 people, 10 have it; 8 of those test positive…". Correct Bayesian answers rose from 4% to 24% in a meta-analysis. (#63)
2. **Absolute risk with the baseline:** "from 2 in 1,000 to 3 in 1,000", not "50% higher". Keep one denominator across comparisons. (#64)
3. **Verbal probability + number** when stakes matter: "likely (~70%)". Readers interpret "likely" as anywhere from 55% to 90%. (#65)
4. **Both frames or neutral counts** for risks: "survive" and "die" frames shift choices. (#79)
5. **Best estimate, then range;** the first number anchors. (#80)
6. **Quantified uncertainty** ("3–5 days") costs little trust; strings of verbal caveats cost more. (#82)
7. **Numerals, not words** ("7", not "seven"). Eyes jump to digits. Split long numbers and IDs into groups of 3–4. (#24, #2)
