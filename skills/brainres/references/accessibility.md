# Accessibility: dyslexia, ADHD, autism, aphasia, older readers, screen readers

These defaults help everyone (the curb-cut effect), so apply them without asking for disclosure. (AN-91)

## Words

- **Use short, frequent, early-learned words.** Gloss at most one domain-only word per sentence, at first use. Expand acronyms at first use. Simplify the words rather than the layout: the core of dyslexia is phonological, not visual. (PL-12, HC-96, AN-73)
- **State instructions literally.** Pair any idiom with its literal meaning. (AN-83)
- **Never replace words with emoji.** Status icons need a text label. (PL-15)

## Sentences and structure

- **Aphasia or low literacy:** sentences of 12 words or fewer, one idea each, the key word in bold, no decorative images or emoji, and no reliance on pictures. (AN-88, HC-95)
- **One instruction per line, no double negatives.** Answers over 400 words start with a summary. Restate information from earlier turns instead of relying on memory. (AN-90)
- **Preview the structure, mark what is required vs optional, and say what happens next.** Autistic readers show elevated intolerance of uncertainty. (AN-84)
- **Match the user's directness and skip rapport padding.** Explicit content transfers equally well across neurotypes. (AN-85)

## Older readers

- **One idea per sentence, at most 5 steps per block, no time-pressure wording.** (AN-55)
- **Keep phrasing familiar and word order normal.** (AN-58)
- **Reduce load, not vocabulary.** No condescension. (AN-59)
- **Put risk information first and make it explicit.** (AN-60)

## Emphasis and markup

- **Use bold for emphasis.** Avoid italic or underline emphasis, and all-caps runs over 2 words. (AN-74)
- **Long answers use real `#` headings, not bold lines posing as headings.** 71.6% of screen-reader users navigate by headings. (HC-111)
- **No decorative emoji bullets.** Screen readers read each emoji's full name. (HC-110)
- **Follow every ASCII diagram with a prose or list equivalent.** (AN-94)
- **Tables always have a header row, at most 5 columns, and no blank cells that carry meaning.** (AN-95)

## Diagrams (round 2)

- The prose carries the full claim, and the diagram is referenced where it's needed. Most readers barely look at illustrations. (DG-07)
