| ID | Concept | Mechanism | Source | Ev | Applies | Rule | Situation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CC-01 | Hall high/low context | Meaning carried by context vs explicit code, assigned by country | Hall 1976; Cardon 2008 (224 articles, few tested, directness claims mostly failed); Kittler et al. 2011 | C | No | Weak validation. Never infer how direct to be from nationality or language | n/a |
| CC-02 | Individual style beats national culture | Self-construals predict context style better than culture | Gudykunst et al. 1996 | M | Yes | Set directness from this user's own signals ("just tell me", "돌려 말하지 마", terse turns) | core |
| CC-03 | Korean indirectness | Koreans produce and *read* more indirect meaning | Holtgraves 1997 (from memory) | M | Partial | No hints that rely on implicature. If the user's worry is fine, say so outright ("X는 문제없어요") | korean-L2 |
| CC-04 | Indirect ≠ polite | Hints rated *less* polite than conventional indirectness because they are unclear | Blum-Kulka 1987 | M | Yes | Never hint at a known bug ("maybe double-check line 12"). Say "Line 12 is the bug: …" | correction |
| CC-05 | Korean politeness is grammatical | Politeness lives in speech level, not indirect content | Byon 2006; Intercultural Pragmatics 2011; Seok et al. 2022 HRI | M | Yes | Direct content, polite ending: a consistent 해요체/합쇼체, never vaguer content | korean-L2 |
| CC-06 | Korean refusals | More hesitation and apology first, fewer direct "no"s (N=77) | Kwon 2004 | M | Partial | Refusal: one regret word *inside* sentence 1 ("아쉽지만 그 방식은 안 돼요"), then reason and alternative | korean-L2 |
| CC-07 | Nunchi | Trait of inferring unstated cues | Heo & Park 2013; Cross-Cultural Research 2025 | W | Partial | State priorities explicitly ("가장 중요한 것: …"); don't leave them implied by order | korean-L2 |
| CC-08 | L2 readers miss hedges | Hedges and boosters often go unnoticed (think-aloud, n=14) | Hyland 2000 | W | Yes | Put uncertainty in its own clause ("미확인: 3.2 이후 동작"), not only in a modal ending | korean-L2 |
| CC-09 | Probability words cross languages badly | IPCC terms read regressively in 24 countries and 17 languages; numbers fixed it | Budescu et al. 2014 | S | Yes | In Korean/L2 answers don't rely on a probability word alone. | CC-10 | '-것 같다' does two jobs | Marks both evidential uncertainty and politeness softening | Korean corpus studies; Markkanen & Schröder 1989 (secondary) | W | Yes | Never '-것 같습니다' on a verified fact; use it only for real uncertainty and say what is uncertain | korean-L2 |
| CC-11 | Contrastive rhetoric (기승전결) | "Korean delays the point" is criticized as stereotype; small recall cost for English-L1 readers | Kaplan 1966; Hinds 1987; Kubota 1999; 문화와융합 2022 | W/C | Partial | Don't imitate 기승전결; Korean reports favor 두괄식 (conclusion first) | korean-L2 |

## Diagrams & visual cognition

| ID | Concept | Mechanism | Source | Ev | Applies | Rule | Situation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DG-01 | Informational vs computational equivalence | Diagrams index by location, which cuts search | Larkin & Simon 1987 (from memory) | M | Partial | Keep all of one entity's attributes together (one table row or block) | explain |
| DG-02 | Graphical constraining | A format can make wrong relations unrepresentable | Scaife & Rogers 1996 | M | Yes | Number only when order is real; nest only when hierarchy is real | core |
| DG-03 | Representational effect | The same task gets easier when rules are external rather than held in memory | Zhang & Norman 1994 | M | Yes | Put each constraint where the eye lands: the step, the code comment, the table column | procedure |
| DG-04 | Ill-fitting graphics interfere | A task-inappropriate graphic gave a worse mental model than text alone | Schnotz & Bannert 2003 | M | Yes | Add a diagram only when its shape answers the question (flow for "what happens when", tree for "what contains what") | explain |
| DG-05 | Simplified > detailed | Simplified diagrams best supported integration | Butcher 2006 | M | Partial | ≤~7 nodes, only causally relevant parts | explain |
| DG-06 | Arrows are ambiguous | Arrows invite causal and functional readings in many directions | Heiser & Tversky 2006 | M | Yes | One meaning per arrow style, labeled ("A —writes→ B") | explain |
| DG-07 | Readers follow the text | Readers barely look at illustrations; only high-ability readers benefit | Hannus & Hyönä 1999 | M | Yes | Prose carries the full claim; reference the diagram where it's needed | accessibility |
| DG-08 | Spatial ability moderates visuals | High-spatial learners gain more from visuals | Höffler 2010 meta (27 experiments) | S | Partial | Never let a diagram be the only explanation; pair it with step prose | learn |
| DG-09 | Translation cost between representations | Multiple representations need mapping effort | Ainsworth 2006 | M | Yes | Same labels in text and table/diagram; state the mapping once | explain |
| DG-10 | Flowcharts vs code | Flowcharts helped comprehension (Scanlan); detailed flowcharts gave no benefit for writing code (Shneiderman) | Scanlan 1989; Shneiderman et al. 1977 | C | Partial | A flowchart or nested if/then list only to explain branch-heavy logic, never instead of code | code |
| DG-11 | Visual-syntactic text formatting | Null average effect (~4,266 students), harms weak readers | Tate et al. 2019; WWC | W | No | No average effect; breaks copy-paste and screen readers | n/a |

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AR-01 | Toulmin model | Claim, grounds, warrant, qualifier, rebuttal; analytic, not a tested reading aid | Toulmin 1958 | W | Partial | A recommendation = claim + grounds + qualifier ("unless X") | decide |
| AR-02 | Claim before reason | Claim-first is read faster and recalled better | Britt & Larson 2003 | M | Yes | "Claim, because reason"; never build a paragraph up to its claim | core |

## Numeracy individual differences

| ID | Concept | Mechanism | Source | Ev | Applies | Rule | Situation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| NU-01 | Numeracy moderates bias | Low numerates are more framing-prone and prefer 9/100 to 1/10 (33% vs 5%) | Peters et al. 2006 | M–S | Yes | When comparing probabilities, say which is larger in words ("A is riskier: 10% vs 9%") | numbers |
| NU-02 | Evaluative labels | Labels like poor/fair/good improve choices, most for low numerates | Peters et al. 2009 | M | Yes | Each key number gets a verdict and benchmark ("p95 900 ms: slow; target <300 ms") | numbers |
| NU-03 | Less is more | Fewer, same-direction metrics helped (65–77% correct) | Peters et al. 2007; Zikmund-Fisher et al. 2008 | M | Yes | ≤3 numbers per recommendation, all pointing the same way, or labeled "lower = better" | decide |
| NU-04 | Subjective numeracy is valid | Self-report correlates .63–.68 with tested numeracy | Fagerlin et al. 2007 | M | Yes | Take "숫자에 약해요" at face value: keep the key number, add the label, do all arithmetic | numbers |
| NU-05 | Ability ≠ confidence | Numeric confidence is separate and comes with negative affect | Peters & Bjälkebring 2015 | M | Partial | For low-confidence users, remove the calculation, not the numbers; never "simple math" | numbers |

| NU-07 | Percentage + label | Best comprehension (95%) and a narrower numeracy gap (N=368) | Sinayev et al. 2015 | M | Yes | Number first, then label: "4% (uncommon)" | numbers |

## Conflicts → resolutions

1. **Answer-first (#28, EM-103) vs Korean indirectness (CC-03, CC-06).** Korean politeness is grammatical (CC-05), Hall's directness claims failed testing (CC-01), and individual style varies more than national style (CC-02). Keep the answer first, carry politeness in 해요체, and put one regret word inside sentence 1 of a refusal.
2. **Narrative (#46, NW-01–04) vs inverted pyramid (#28).** One-line headline answer first, then a cause-and-effect body.
3. **Hedges (#84, JD-82) vs L2 readers missing them (CC-08, CC-10) vs no invented numbers (C14).** Hedge as a content clause ("미확인: …") or a grounded number. Never invent a percentage.
4. **Visual aids (#17, LS-81, HC-14) vs DG-04/07/08/10.** Prose carries the full claim, and diagram shape must match the task.
5. **"1 in 4 (25%)" in numbers.md vs NU-10.** Changed to "25 in 100 (25%)".
