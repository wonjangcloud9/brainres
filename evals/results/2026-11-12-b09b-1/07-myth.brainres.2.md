<!-- Q: Humans only use 10% of their brains, right? -->

No, that's a myth. Humans use virtually all of their brain, just not all of it at the same moment.

Brain imaging (fMRI, PET) shows activity across every region over the course of a day, even during sleep. Damage to almost any area causes some deficit, which wouldn't happen if 90% were unused. The brain is also about 2% of body weight but uses roughly 20% of the body's energy, and evolution wouldn't maintain such an expensive organ if most of it sat idle.

The myth probably persists from misread early neuroscience, self-help marketing, and the fact that only a fraction of neurons fire at any one instant. That's efficient signaling, not unused capacity.

If you want a source to check, search for "the 10 percent myth" in Scientific American or the Barry Beyerstein chapter in *Mind Myths* (1999).
