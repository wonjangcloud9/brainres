<!-- Q: A test for a disease is 99% accurate and 1 in 1,000 people have it. I tested positive. How likely is it that I actually have it? -->

About 9%, assuming "99% accurate" means the test catches 99% of sick people and clears 99% of healthy people.

Take 100,000 people:

- 100 have the disease, and 99 of them test positive.
- 99,900 are healthy, and 1% of them (999) test positive by mistake.
- That makes 99 + 999 = 1,098 positives, and only 99 are real: 99 / 1,098 ≈ **9%**.

The disease is rare, so false positives from the large healthy group outnumber true positives about 10 to 1. If the test's sensitivity and specificity differ from 99%, the number changes, so check them on the test's documentation.

Next step: ask for a confirmatory test. A second independent positive would raise your odds to roughly 91%.
