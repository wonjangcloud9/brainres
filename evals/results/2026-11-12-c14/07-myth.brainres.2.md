<!-- Q: Humans only use 10% of their brains, right? -->

No, that's a myth. Humans use virtually all of their brain, just not all of it at the same moment.

Brain imaging (fMRI, PET) shows activity across the whole brain over a day, and even during sleep many regions stay active. Damage to almost any area causes some deficit, which wouldn't happen if 90% were unused. The brain is also about 2% of body weight but burns roughly 20% of the body's energy, so evolution would not have kept a mostly idle organ that costly.

The myth likely persists because of misread early neuroscience and self-help marketing. One real kernel: at any given moment only a fraction of neurons fire, which is efficient, since firing them all at once would resemble a seizure.
