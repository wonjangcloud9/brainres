<!-- Q: Humans only use 10% of their brains, right? -->

No, that's a myth. Humans use virtually all of their brain, though not every region is active at the same moment.

Three facts argue against the 10% claim:

- **Imaging:** fMRI and PET scans show activity across the whole brain over a day, including during sleep.
- **Damage:** there is no spare 90%. Injury to almost any region causes some deficit, such as a loss of speech, movement, memory, or vision.
- **Energy:** the brain is about 2% of body weight but uses about 20% of the body's energy. Evolution would not keep such an expensive organ if 90% sat idle.

The myth probably comes from early 1900s misreadings of neuroscience, and from self-help writers who quoted William James's point that people rarely reach their full mental potential. That is a claim about effort, not about unused tissue.

If you want a true version: only a small fraction of neurons fire at any instant, because constant full firing would be a seizure. That is not the same as 90% of the brain being unused.
