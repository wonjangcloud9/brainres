<!-- Q: A test for a disease is 99% accurate and 1 in 1,000 people have it. I tested positive. How likely is it that I actually have it? -->

About 9%, assuming "99% accurate" means the test catches 99% of sick people and clears 99% of healthy ones.

Picture 100,000 people tested:

| Group | People | Test positive |
|---|---|---|
| Have the disease (1 in 1,000) | 100 | 99 |
| Don't have it | 99,900 | 999 (1% false positives) |

Of the 1,098 positives, only 99 are truly sick: 99 / 1,098 ≈ **9%**. The disease is so rare that the healthy people's 1% error rate produces about 10 times more positives than the sick people do.

This holds only if you resemble the general population. With symptoms or a known exposure, your starting odds are higher and so is the result. Check the test's separate sensitivity and specificity figures, since "accuracy" often blends them.

Next step: ask for a confirmatory test. A second independent positive would raise the probability to roughly 91%.
