<!-- Q: I'm not technical. What is 'the cloud'? -->

"The cloud" means computers owned by someone else, usually a big company, that you use over the internet instead of keeping everything on your own device.

**How it works**

These companies run huge buildings called **data centers**. Each one is full of powerful computers called **servers**. When you use a cloud service, your files or apps live on those servers. Your phone or laptop just reaches them through the internet. The name "cloud" is only a metaphor because you never see the machines.

**Examples you probably already use**

- **Photos:** iCloud Photos or Google Photos keep your pictures on their servers. So if you lose your phone, the photos survive.
- **Email:** Gmail stores your messages on Google's servers. That's why you can read them from any device.
- **Streaming:** Netflix and Spotify send shows and music from their servers as you watch or listen.
- **Documents:** Google Docs or Microsoft 365 let you edit a file from any computer.

**The trade-off**

- **Benefit:** You can reach your stuff anywhere, and it stays safe if your device breaks.
- **Cost:** You need an internet connection, and you're trusting a company to keep your data private and secure.

You're on a Mac, so you can see your own cloud storage. Open **System Settings** and click your name at the top. Then click **iCloud** to see what's stored there.
