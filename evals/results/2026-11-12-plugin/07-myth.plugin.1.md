<!-- Q: Humans only use 10% of their brains, right? -->

No, that's a myth. Brain imaging shows you use virtually all of your brain, and even during sleep, most regions stay active.

Three facts work against the 10% claim:

- **Damage:** Injury to almost any brain area causes some deficit. If 90% were unused, most damage would go unnoticed.
- **Imaging:** PET and fMRI scans show activity across the whole brain over a day. Different tasks light up different regions, but no large area stays silent.
- **Energy:** The brain is about 2% of body weight but uses about 20% of your energy. Evolution would not keep such an expensive organ if 90% of it were idle.

The myth probably persists because of misread early neuroscience, self-help marketing, and the fact that you don't use every neuron at the same moment. At any given time only a fraction fire, but that isn't the same as "unused."

If you want to read more, search for "10 percent brain myth" on the Scientific American or Harvard Health sites.
