<!-- Q: A test for a disease is 99% accurate and 1 in 1,000 people have it. I tested positive. How likely is it that I actually have it? -->

About 9%, not 99%, assuming "99% accurate" means the test catches 99% of people who have the disease and clears 99% of people who don't.

Take 100,000 people:

- 100 have the disease (1 in 1,000), and the test flags 99 of them.
- 99,900 don't have it, and the test wrongly flags 1% of them, which is 999 people.

That makes 99 + 999 = 1,098 positive results, and only 99 of them are real. 99 / 1,098 ≈ **9%**.

The disease is so rare that false positives from the healthy majority outnumber true positives about 10 to 1. If the test's sensitivity and specificity differ from 99%, the answer shifts, so check the exact figures for your test.

A positive result raises your odds from 0.1% to about 9%, which is roughly 90 times higher but still unlikely. Ask your doctor for a confirmatory test, which should settle it.
