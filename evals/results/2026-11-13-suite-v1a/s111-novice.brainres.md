<!-- Q: I'm not technical. What is 'the cloud'? -->

"The cloud" is just other people's computers, owned by companies like Amazon, Google, or Apple, that you reach over the internet instead of using the one on your desk.

When you save a photo to iCloud or open a Google Doc, the file isn't living on your laptop. It sits on a computer in a big warehouse (a "data center") somewhere, and your device fetches it when you ask.

Why people use it:

- **Access anywhere:** your files show up on your phone, laptop, or a friend's computer once you log in.
- **Safe from accidents:** if your laptop breaks or is stolen, your files are still there.
- **Less to manage:** the company handles the hardware, updates, and backups.

The trade-offs are that you need internet to reach your files, and you're trusting the company with your data, so a strong password and two-step login matter.

If you want to see it in your own life, check whether your phone backs up photos automatically. That's the cloud at work.
