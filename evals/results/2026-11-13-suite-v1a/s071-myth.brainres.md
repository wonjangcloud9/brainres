<!-- Q: Is it true that we only use 10% of our brain? -->

No, it's a myth: brain imaging shows we use virtually all of the brain, just not every region at the same moment.

- **Imaging:** fMRI and PET scans show activity across the whole brain over a day, even during sleep.
- **Damage:** almost any brain injury causes some deficit. If 90% were unused, most injuries would go unnoticed.
- **Energy:** the brain is about 2% of body weight but uses about 20% of the body's energy. Evolution would not keep such an expensive organ mostly idle.
- **Neurons:** unused neurons die off, and no region of the brain is found to be silent.

The myth likely comes from early 1900s misquotes of psychologist William James, who said people rarely reach their full mental potential. That claim was about performance, not brain tissue.

If you want a source to check, look up neuroscientist Barry Beyerstein's "Whence Cometh the Myth that We Only Use 10% of Our Brains?"
