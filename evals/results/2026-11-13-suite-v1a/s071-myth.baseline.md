<!-- Q: Is it true that we only use 10% of our brain? -->

No, that's a myth.

- **Brain imaging** (fMRI, PET) shows activity throughout the whole brain over the course of a day. Even during sleep, many regions are active.
- **Damage studies**: if 90% of the brain were unused, damage to most areas would cause no effect. In reality, damage to almost any region causes some deficit.
- **Energy cost**: the brain is about 2% of body weight but uses roughly 20% of the body's energy. Evolution wouldn't maintain such an expensive organ if 90% of it sat idle.
- **Localization**: we've mapped functions to nearly every part of the brain, and no large "silent" area exists.

**Where the myth may come from:** early neuroscience misinterpretations, a misquote of William James (who said people rarely reach their full mental potential), and the fact that only a fraction of neurons fire at any given moment. That's normal and efficient, not evidence of unused capacity. Glial cells outnumber neurons in some estimates, which may have also fueled confusion.

You use all of your brain, just not all of it at the same time.
