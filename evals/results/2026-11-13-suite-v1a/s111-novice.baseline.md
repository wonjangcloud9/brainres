<!-- Q: I'm not technical. What is 'the cloud'? -->

"The cloud" is just a friendly name for **other people's computers that you use over the internet**.

**The basic idea**

Instead of storing your files or running programs on your own laptop or phone, you use powerful computers owned by a company (like Apple, Google, Amazon, or Microsoft). Those computers sit in big buildings called data centers. You connect to them through the internet.

**Everyday examples you probably already use**

- **Photos on your phone backed up to iCloud or Google Photos.** They're stored on the company's computers, so you can still see them if you lose your phone.
- **Gmail.** Your emails live on Google's computers, not on your device. That's why you can read them from any phone or laptop.
- **Netflix or Spotify.** The movies and music are stored elsewhere and sent to you when you press play.
- **Google Docs.** The document is saved online, and several people can edit it at once.

**Why people use it**

- Access your stuff from any device
- Automatic backups if your device breaks
- Easy sharing with others
- Companies can rent as much computing power as they need, rather than buying their own equipment

**The trade-offs**

- You generally need an internet connection
- You're trusting the company to keep your data safe and private
- Many services charge a monthly fee for storage

**Why the name?**

Engineers used to draw a cloud shape in diagrams to represent "the internet, somewhere out there." The name stuck, even though there's nothing fluffy about it. It's just real computers in real buildings.
