<!-- Q: Humans only use 10% of their brains, right? -->

No, that's a myth. You use virtually all of your brain.

- **Brain imaging (fMRI, PET)** shows activity across essentially every region over the course of a day. Different tasks light up different areas, but no region sits permanently idle.
- **Damage anywhere has consequences.** If 90% were unused, most strokes or injuries would be harmless. In reality, damage to almost any area causes some deficit.
- **The brain is metabolically expensive.** It's about 2% of body weight but uses roughly 20% of the body's energy. Evolution wouldn't maintain that much costly tissue doing nothing.
- **Neurons that go unused tend to weaken or be pruned**, so a large dormant reserve wouldn't persist.

There's a small grain of truth: at any single instant, only a fraction of neurons are firing. That's by design, since everything firing at once would be a seizure. But that's very different from 90% of the brain never being used.

The myth probably grew from misquoted early psychology (William James wrote that people use only a fraction of their *potential*), early uncertainty about what glial cells and "silent" cortex did, and later self-help marketing. Movies like *Lucy* and *Limitless* keep it going.
