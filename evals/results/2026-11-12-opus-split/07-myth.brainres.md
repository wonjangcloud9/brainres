<!-- Q: Humans only use 10% of their brains, right? -->

No, that's a myth: you use virtually all of your brain, though not all of it at the same moment.

The evidence against it is strong:

- **Brain imaging:** fMRI and PET scans show activity across every region over a normal day. Even during sleep, no area stays permanently dark.
- **Brain damage:** No region can be damaged without some effect. If 90% were idle, most strokes would be harmless.
- **Energy cost:** The brain is about 2% of body weight but uses roughly 20% of resting energy. Evolution wouldn't sustain that much unused tissue.

The myth likely grew from misquoted early-1900s psychology about untapped potential. Movies like *Lucy* and *Limitless* keep it alive. If you want a quick source, search "10 percent brain myth Scientific American."
