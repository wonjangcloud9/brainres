<!-- Q: My laptop won't turn on and my thesis is due tomorrow morning. -->

Your thesis file is most likely safe, so get to a working computer and look for it in the cloud before you try to fix the laptop.

1. **Check cloud copies on any other device.** Try Google Docs, iCloud, OneDrive, Dropbox, Overleaf, and your university email, including drafts you sent yourself. Version history often holds a recent copy.
2. **Test the power.** Hold the power button for 15 seconds, then try a different charger and outlet. Leave it plugged in for 10 minutes, then press the power button. A dead battery causes many "won't turn on" cases.
3. **Mac only:** Apple silicon: hold the power button for 10 seconds. Intel: press Shift+Control+Option+Power for 10 seconds. Then try turning it on again.
4. **If it still won't start, pull the files off the drive.**
   - Take it to an Apple Store or repair shop and ask for a data transfer. Many do this the same day.
   - On a Windows laptop with a removable SSD, a shop can put it in an enclosure so you can read it from another machine.
5. **Plan for the worst.** Email your advisor now and ask for a short extension, or ask for the deadline to be moved to the afternoon. Advisors usually grant this for hardware failure, and asking early helps.

The risk is the files, not the laptop. Don't open the case or try repairs yourself until the thesis is copied somewhere else.
