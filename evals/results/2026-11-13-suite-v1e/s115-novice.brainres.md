<!-- Q: My grandmother asked me what an algorithm is. How do I explain it to her? -->

Tell her an algorithm is a recipe: a list of exact steps that turns ingredients into a finished dish, the same way every time.

**What to say**

"Grandma, when you make kimchi stew, you follow steps you know by heart. You do them in order, and you get the same stew each time. An algorithm is just a set of steps like that, written so clearly that even a computer can follow it."

**A second example she may like**

Finding a name in a phone book:
1. Open the book to the middle.
2. If the name comes earlier in the alphabet, look at the left half. If later, look at the right half.
3. Repeat with the half you chose until you find the name.

She probably already does this without thinking. Point out that she was running an algorithm.

**Connecting it to her life**

- When her phone suggests a video or a route, a recipe of steps picked it.
- The computer doesn't "think". It follows the steps very fast and never skips one.

**Two tips**

- Skip words like "input", "logic" and "code". "Steps" and "recipe" are enough.
- Ask her to tell you her recipe step by step. When she does, you can say, "That's an algorithm."

Try the recipe version first, and bring up the phone book only if she wants another example.
