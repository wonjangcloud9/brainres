<!-- Q: My laptop won't turn on and my thesis is due tomorrow morning. -->

That's stressful. Let's get you to your thesis first and fix the laptop second.

**Check where your thesis already lives (2 minutes):**
- **Cloud sync:** Google Docs/Drive, OneDrive, iCloud, Dropbox, or Overleaf. Log in from any other device or a library computer.
- **Email:** Search your sent mail and drafts for attachments you sent yourself or your advisor.
- **Version history:** Word/Pages autosave to OneDrive/iCloud, and Overleaf keeps history.
- **Backups:** Time Machine drive, external disk, or USB stick.

If it's there, borrow any computer and keep working. The laptop can wait.

**If the files are only on the dead laptop, try to revive it:**
1. **Check power:** Try a different outlet and cable, and look for a charging light. Leave it plugged in for 15-30 minutes, since a fully drained battery can take a while to respond.
2. **Hard reset:** Unplug everything, hold the power button for 30-60 seconds, then plug in the charger and press power.
   - **Windows:** Remove the battery too, if it's removable.
   - **Mac (Apple silicon):** Hold the power button for 10 seconds, then try again. Intel Macs may need an SMC reset.
3. **Check for signs of life:** Fans, keyboard backlight, or a startup chime or beep. If the screen is black but the laptop seems on, try an external monitor or turning the brightness up.
4. **Recover the data if it won't boot:**
   - **Mac:** Target Disk Mode (Intel) or Share Disk in recovery (Apple silicon) with a second Mac.
   - **Windows:** Remove the SSD and use a USB enclosure (~$15) on another computer.
   - A local repair shop can often do this same day.

**Meanwhile, protect your deadline:**
- Email your advisor or department now, briefly, saying you have a hardware failure and asking about a short extension. Do it early, not at the last minute.
- Many universities have emergency loaner laptops. Check the library or IT help desk.
