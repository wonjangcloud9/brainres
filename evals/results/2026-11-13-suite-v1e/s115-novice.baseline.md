<!-- Q: My grandmother asked me what an algorithm is. How do I explain it to her? -->

An algorithm is just a recipe: a list of clear steps you follow to get something done.

**Start with something she already knows.** Her favorite recipe works well. Ingredients go in, you follow the steps in order, and out comes a cake. If the steps are clear enough, anyone can follow them and get the same result. That's an algorithm.

**A few more everyday examples:**
- Directions to her house: "Turn left at the church, go two blocks, it's the blue door."
- Knitting or sewing patterns.
- How she sorts laundry: whites here, darks there, delicates by hand.
- Looking up a name in a phone book: open to the middle, decide whether you went too far or not far enough, and repeat on the right half.

**Then connect it to computers.** A computer can't guess or use common sense, so it needs every step spelled out exactly. An algorithm is how people write those instructions. When she searches for something online, or her phone suggests a photo, or a video site recommends a show, a recipe (an algorithm) is running behind the scenes.

**If she's heard the word in the news:** people often say "the algorithm" about social media. You can say that's a recipe the company wrote to decide what to show her next, based on what she's looked at before.

**A one-sentence version:** "An algorithm is a set of step-by-step instructions for solving a problem, like a recipe, except the cook is a computer."

If she likes hands-on explanations, you could ask her to give you instructions for making a cup of tea, then follow them *literally* (for example, "put the tea in the cup" with the bag still in its box). It's a fun way to show why the steps have to be precise.
