<!-- Q: Should I learn Rust or Go next? -->

**Go** if you want to be productive fast; **Rust** if you want to learn something that changes how you think about programming.

**Pick Go if:**
- You're building web services, APIs, CLIs, or infrastructure/devops tooling
- You want to ship something useful within a week or two
- You value simplicity and a small language you can fully hold in your head
- Your job market leans toward cloud/backend (Kubernetes, Docker, Terraform ecosystems are Go)

**Pick Rust if:**
- You care about systems programming, performance-critical code, embedded, WebAssembly, or security-sensitive software
- You're okay with a steep learning curve (ownership and borrowing take weeks to click)
- You want to deepen your understanding of memory, concurrency, and type systems, which carries over to every other language
- You enjoy expressive types, pattern matching, and having the compiler catch whole classes of bugs

**My default recommendation:** if you don't have a specific goal pulling you toward systems work, start with Go. It's quick to learn, and you'll get real output right away. Then pick up Rust later when you hit a problem where you need its guarantees or performance. Going Go → Rust is a smoother path than the reverse.
