<!-- Q: Humans only use 10% of their brains, right? -->

No, that's a myth: humans use essentially all of their brain, just not all of it at the same moment.

Brain imaging (fMRI, PET) shows activity across the whole brain over the course of a day, even during sleep. Damage to almost any region causes some deficit, which wouldn't happen if 90% were unused. The brain is also about 2% of body weight but burns roughly 20% of the body's energy, and evolution wouldn't maintain that cost for idle tissue.

The myth likely comes from misread early neuroscience, such as the finding that only a small fraction of neurons fire at any instant (which is normal and efficient), plus self-help marketing.

If you want to check it yourself, search for "fMRI whole-brain activity" or look up how stroke in different regions causes different impairments.
