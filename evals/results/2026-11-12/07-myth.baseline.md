<!-- Q: Humans only use 10% of their brains, right? -->

No, that's a myth. Humans use essentially all of their brain, just not all of it at the same moment.

- **Imaging shows broad activity.** fMRI and PET scans show activity throughout the brain over the course of a day. Even during sleep, many regions are active.
- **Damage matters almost anywhere.** If 90% of the brain were unused, most injuries would be harmless. In reality, damage to nearly any area causes some deficit, whether in movement, memory, language, perception, or personality.
- **Evolution argues against it.** The brain uses about 20% of the body's energy while being roughly 2% of its mass. It's unlikely natural selection would maintain such an expensive organ if 90% were idle.
- **Neurons that go unused die off.** Unused circuits get pruned, so large dormant regions wouldn't persist.
- **Localization studies.** Mapping has assigned functions to essentially every region, with no large "silent" area.

**Where the myth may come from:** early neuroscience misreadings (for example, that most brain cells are glial cells, which was misinterpreted as "unused"), misquoted statements by William James about untapped human potential, and self-help marketing.

A grain of truth: at any given moment only a fraction of neurons fire, and that's by design. If all fired at once, you'd have something like a seizure.
