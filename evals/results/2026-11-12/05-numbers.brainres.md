<!-- Q: A test for a disease is 99% accurate and 1 in 1,000 people have it. I tested positive. How likely is it that I actually have it? -->

About **9%**, not 99%, assuming "99% accurate" means 99% sensitivity and 99% specificity.

The reason is that the disease is rare, so false positives outnumber true positives. Take 100,000 people:

- 100 have the disease, and the test catches 99 of them.
- 99,900 don't have it, and 1% of them (999) test positive anyway.
- Total positives: 99 + 999 = 1,098, of which 99 are real. 99 / 1,098 ≈ **9%**.

If the test's sensitivity and specificity differ from 99% each, the number shifts, so check the test's package insert or ask your doctor for both figures. Also, if you had symptoms or a risk factor that made you more likely than 1 in 1,000 before testing, your real chance is higher than 9%.

Next step: ask for a confirmatory test. A second independent positive would raise your odds to roughly 90%.
