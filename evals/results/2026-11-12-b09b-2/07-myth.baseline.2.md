<!-- Q: Humans only use 10% of their brains, right? -->

No, that's a myth. Humans use virtually all of their brain, just not all regions at the same moment.

- **Imaging shows broad activity.** fMRI and PET scans show activity across the whole brain over the course of a day, even during sleep. No large region is permanently dormant.
- **Damage is costly everywhere.** If 90% of the brain were unused, most injuries would be harmless. In reality, damage to almost any area causes some deficit.
- **Energy cost.** The brain is about 2% of body weight but uses roughly 20% of the body's energy. Evolution wouldn't maintain such an expensive organ if 90% of it sat idle.
- **Anatomy and function.** Every region has been mapped to functions like movement, vision, language, memory, and emotion. Neurons that go unused tend to wither.

The myth probably persists because of a few things: misquoted early psychology, such as William James saying people don't reach their full mental potential; the fact that only a small fraction of neurons fire at any instant (which is normal and efficient); and its appeal in movies like *Lucy* and *Limitless*.
