<!-- Q: Is it worth getting AWS certifications in 2026? -->

Short answer: it depends on your situation, but for many people a cert is worth it as a *supporting* credential, rarely as the main thing.

**Where it's worth it**
- **Breaking in or switching into cloud/DevOps/infra roles.** A cert (Cloud Practitioner → Solutions Architect Associate) helps get past resume filters and shows structured knowledge when you lack job history.
- **Consulting, MSPs, and AWS partner companies.** Partners need certified headcount for AWS partner tiers, so employers often require or pay for them, and they can affect your rate.
- **Government, enterprise, and large-company hiring.** These tend to value formal credentials more.
- **Employer pays.** If the exam and study time are covered, the cost/benefit is easy.
- **You learn better with a structured syllabus.** The exam path forces breadth you might skip otherwise.

**Where it's less valuable**
- **You already have 3+ years of hands-on AWS experience.** Your work history says more than a cert does.
- **Startups and product-focused engineering roles.** They usually care more about what you've built and operated.
- **Collecting entry-level certs.** Past one or two, the returns drop fast. Cloud Practitioner alone does little for hiring.
- **Cert-only, no projects.** Hiring managers are skeptical of people who can pass exams but can't debug an IAM policy or design a VPC.

**Practical advice**
1. **Best value per effort:** Solutions Architect Associate (SAA). It's broadly recognized and teaches a lot.
2. **Specialty/Professional certs** (Solutions Architect Pro, Security Specialty, DevOps Pro) are worth it mainly if you're in that specialty or your employer values them.
3. **Pair it with a project.** Deploy something real (IaC with Terraform or CDK, CI/CD, monitoring) and put it on your resume next to the cert.
4. **Check job postings** for the roles you want. If they list AWS certs frequently, it's worth it. If not, skip it.
5. **AI-era note:** With AI tools generating a lot of boilerplate infra code, employers increasingly value design judgment, cost awareness, security, and debugging. Certs partially cover this, but only if you actually learn the material rather than memorize dumps.

**Costs:** exams are roughly $100 (Practitioner/Associate level) to $300 (Pro/Specialty), plus study materials. Certs are valid for 3 years, so factor in renewal.
