<!-- Q: Does more RAM always make a computer faster? -->

No. More RAM only speeds things up when the computer is running out of it, so extra RAM beyond what your workload needs changes nothing.

**When it helps:** If you have many apps or browser tabs open, or you edit large video or datasets, the system runs short of RAM. It then swaps data to the disk, which is far slower. Adding RAM removes that swapping, and the computer feels much faster.

**When it doesn't:** If you already have free RAM while working, more adds nothing. Speed then depends on the CPU, the GPU, or the storage drive. A slow CPU or an old hard drive stays slow regardless of RAM.

**How to check on your Mac:** Open Activity Monitor, go to the Memory tab, and look at "Memory Pressure". Green means you have enough. Yellow or red, or a large "Swap Used" value, means more RAM would help.

Check that graph during your heaviest normal work before buying anything.
