<!-- Q: Does more RAM always make a computer faster? -->

No. More RAM only helps if your computer is actually running short of it.

**When more RAM helps**
- You regularly have many apps, browser tabs, or large files open and the system starts swapping (using disk as overflow memory), which is much slower.
- You do memory-heavy work: video editing, large datasets, virtual machines, big code builds, modern games.

**When it doesn't**
- You already have free RAM most of the time. Extra capacity just sits unused.
- The bottleneck is elsewhere: a slow CPU, an old hard drive (an SSD upgrade helps far more), a weak GPU, or a slow network.
- Software that is poorly optimized or limited to a single core won't get faster with more memory.

**Other factors**
- RAM speed and channel configuration (dual-channel vs. single) can matter more than capacity once you have enough.
- On a Mac like yours (Apple silicon uses unified memory that can't be upgraded later), it's worth picking the right amount at purchase, but beyond what your workload needs there's little benefit.

**How to check if you need more**
On macOS, open Activity Monitor → Memory tab and look at **Memory Pressure**. Green means you're fine, yellow or red, along with high swap usage, suggests more RAM would help.
