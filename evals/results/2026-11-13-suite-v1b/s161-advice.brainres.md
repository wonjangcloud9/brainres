<!-- Q: Is it worth getting AWS certifications in 2026? -->

Yes, for some people: it pays off most if you're early-career, switching into cloud, or work at an AWS partner or consultancy, and much less if you already have 3+ years of hands-on AWS work.

**Where it helps**
- **Early-career or career-switchers:** a cert gets your résumé past filters when you have little production experience.
- **AWS partners and consultancies:** partner tiers often require a set number of certified staff, so employers value and sometimes pay for them.
- **Structured learning:** the exam syllabus pushes you through services you wouldn't touch otherwise.

**Where it helps less**
- **Experienced engineers:** hiring managers weigh projects, incident stories and system design over a badge. A cert alone rarely beats a strong portfolio or referral.
- **Fast-moving services:** exam content lags behind new services, especially AI and ML offerings, so a cert signals baseline knowledge rather than current skill.

**Which ones to pick**

| Goal | Cert |
|---|---|
| Entry into cloud | Solutions Architect Associate |
| Platform/DevOps roles | DevOps Engineer Professional or SysOps/CloudOps Associate |
| Senior architecture | Solutions Architect Professional |
| Non-technical roles | Cloud Practitioner (low value for engineers) |

I can't verify 2026 exam prices or any recent lineup changes, so check the AWS Certification site before you commit. Also ask whether your employer reimburses the exam fee, which makes the cost-benefit much easier.
