"The cloud" is just other people's computers, owned by big companies like Amazon, Google, and Microsoft, that you reach over the internet.

Normally, your photos, documents, and apps live on your own device. With the cloud, they live in a company's huge buildings full of computers (called data centers). Your device asks for them when you need them.

**Everyday examples:**
- Photos that show up on both your phone and laptop (iCloud, Google Photos)
- Gmail, which you read in a browser without it being stored on your computer
- Netflix, which streams from their computers instead of playing a file you own

**Why people use it:**
1. Your files are available from any device.
2. If your phone breaks, your files are still safe.
3. You don't need to buy or maintain the big computers yourself.

**The tradeoff:** you need internet to reach your stuff, and you are trusting a company to store it securely.

The name comes from old network diagrams, where the internet was drawn as a cloud shape. Nothing floats in the sky.
