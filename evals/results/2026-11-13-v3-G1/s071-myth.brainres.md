<!-- Q: Is it true that we only use 10% of our brain? -->

No, it's a myth. You use essentially all of your brain, just not all of it at the same moment.

- **Imaging:** fMRI and PET scans show activity across the whole brain over a day. Even during sleep, many regions stay active.
- **Damage:** If 90% of the brain were unused, injuries to most areas would cause no harm. In reality, damage to almost any region causes some deficit.
- **Energy:** The brain is about 2% of body weight but uses about 20% of your energy. Evolution would not keep such an expensive organ mostly idle.
- **Anatomy:** Mapping studies have found a function for nearly every region, and unused neurons tend to die off.

The myth probably grew from early 20th-century misreadings of neuroscience, plus self-help writers who liked the idea of untapped potential.

A true version of the idea: at any given moment, only a fraction of neurons fire. That is efficient, not wasteful, since firing them all at once would resemble a seizure.
