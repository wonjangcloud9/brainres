<!-- Q: I'm panicking. I sent the wrong file to a client and it has another client's confidential prices in it. -->

Act in the next few minutes: try to recall the message, then tell your manager, because the notification decision isn't yours alone.

1. **Stop access now.** If you sent a link (Drive, Dropbox, SharePoint), revoke it or change its permission to "no access." If it was an attachment, try Recall (Outlook) or Undo Send (Gmail, only works within seconds to a minute or so after sending).
2. **Tell your manager right now.** Say what was sent, to whom, when, and which client's prices were exposed. Companies often have a data-incident or privacy contact, and your manager will know who that is. Reporting fast protects you more than any fix you attempt alone.
3. **Ask the recipient to delete it.** Send a short, factual note: the file was sent in error, please delete it from email and downloads without opening or sharing it, and reply to confirm. Don't describe the contents. Have your manager approve it first if you can do so quickly.
4. **Write down the facts.** Record the time sent, recipients, file name, what it contained, and what you did, in order.

Whether the other client must be told depends on your NDA and contract terms, so leave that to your manager or legal.
