<!-- Q: Is it true that we only use 10% of our brain? -->

No, it's a myth: we use essentially all of our brain, though not every region is active at the same moment.

Three lines of evidence show this:

- **Imaging:** fMRI and PET scans show activity across the whole brain over a day, even during sleep.
- **Damage:** Almost any brain injury causes some loss of function. If 90% of the brain were unused, most damage would go unnoticed.
- **Energy cost:** The brain is about 2% of body weight but uses about 20% of the body's energy. Evolution would not maintain such an expensive organ if 90% of it sat idle.

The myth probably persists because people misread early neuroscience. Researchers once found that only a small share of neurons fire at any given instant, and that glial cells outnumber neurons in some counts. Both facts are easy to twist into "most of the brain is unused." Self-help marketing then kept the claim alive.

If you want to check this yourself, search for "brain imaging whole-brain activity" in a review such as those by Barry Beyerstein, who wrote on this myth.
