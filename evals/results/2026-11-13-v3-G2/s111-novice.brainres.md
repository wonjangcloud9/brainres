<!-- Q: I'm not technical. What is 'the cloud'? -->

"The cloud" is just other people's computers, owned by companies like Amazon, Google, and Microsoft, that you reach over the internet instead of using the one on your desk.

When you save a photo to iCloud, watch Netflix, or open a Google Doc, the files and programs live on those company computers, which sit in large buildings called data centers. Your phone or laptop only shows you the result.

This matters for three practical reasons:

1. **Access from anywhere:** your files are available on any device where you log in.
2. **Backup:** if your laptop breaks, your files are still on the company's computers.
3. **Renting instead of buying:** a business can pay monthly for as much computer power as it needs, rather than buying its own machines.

The name comes from old network diagrams, where engineers drew a cloud shape to mean "the internet, details not important." It doesn't mean your data floats in the sky.

The tradeoff is that you depend on the company and on your internet connection, so no connection means no access.
