<!-- Q: Is it true that we only use 10% of our brain? -->

No, it's a myth: we use virtually all of our brain, just not every region at the same moment.

The strongest evidence against it:

- **Imaging:** fMRI and PET scans show activity across the whole brain over a day, including during sleep.
- **Damage:** There is no "spare" 90%. Injury to almost any region causes some deficit, such as loss of movement, speech, memory, or vision.
- **Energy cost:** The brain is about 2% of body weight but uses roughly 20% of the body's energy. Evolution wouldn't maintain such an expensive organ if 90% sat idle.
- **Anatomy:** Neurons that go unused die off or get pruned, yet most of the brain remains intact in healthy adults.

The myth likely persists from misquoted early psychology (William James said people use only a fraction of their *potential*), plus the fact that at any instant only a subset of neurons fire strongly. That's efficient coordination, not idle capacity.
