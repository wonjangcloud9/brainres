<!-- Q: I'm not technical. What is 'the cloud'? -->

"The cloud" is a way of saying "someone else's computers, accessed over the internet."

When you use a service like Gmail, Google Docs, Netflix, or iCloud, your files and programs often aren't stored on your phone or laptop. They're stored in large data centers owned by companies like Amazon, Google, or Microsoft. Your device just connects to them over the internet to read, save, or run things.

**Some everyday examples:**
- **Email:** Your messages sit on Gmail's servers, not only on your phone. That's why you can see them on any device.
- **Photos:** iCloud or Google Photos keeps copies of your pictures off your phone.
- **Streaming:** Netflix stores the movies in its data centers and sends them to you on demand.
- **Online documents:** Google Docs saves your work remotely, so you can open it anywhere.

**Why it matters:**
- **Convenience:** Your stuff is available from any device.
- **Backup:** If your phone breaks, your data is still safe elsewhere.
- **Trade-off:** You're trusting another company to keep your data safe and running, and your access depends on an internet connection.

So when someone says "it's in the cloud," they just mean it's stored or running on remote computers instead of the device in front of you.
