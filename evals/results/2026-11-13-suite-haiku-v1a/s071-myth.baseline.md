<!-- Q: Is it true that we only use 10% of our brain? -->

No, that's a myth. We use virtually all of our brain, not 10%.

Brain imaging (fMRI, PET) shows activity across essentially every region over the course of a day, even during sleep. Damage to almost any part of the brain causes some measurable deficit, which wouldn't happen if large portions were unused. And the brain is metabolically expensive, about 20% of the body's energy at rest, which evolution wouldn't sustain for tissue that did nothing.

The myth has several possible origins: misquoted early neuroscience (William James wrote that people use only a fraction of their potential, which is a different claim), self-help marketing, and movies like *Lucy*. It's also sometimes conflated with the idea that only a small fraction of neurons fire at any given moment. That's partly true (neural activity is sparse), but it's not the same as the brain being mostly idle.
