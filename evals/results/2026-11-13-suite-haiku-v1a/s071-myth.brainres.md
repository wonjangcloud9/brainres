<!-- Q: Is it true that we only use 10% of our brain? -->

No. The 10% claim is a myth. Brain imaging (PET and fMRI) shows that nearly all brain regions are active over the course of a day, and even simple tasks engage many areas at once.

The myth likely traces back to early 20th-century misreadings of neuroscience and to self-help marketing. Damage to almost any part of the brain causes some deficit, which would be unlikely if 90% were unused. Neurons also cost a lot of energy: the brain is about 2% of body weight but uses roughly 20% of its oxygen and calories, so the body would not maintain a mostly idle organ.

Your brain is not uniformly active at every moment. At any given time, some networks are more engaged than others, and learning can strengthen particular circuits. That is different from having 90% of it unused.
