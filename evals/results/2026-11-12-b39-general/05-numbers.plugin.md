<!-- Q: A test for a disease is 99% accurate and 1 in 1,000 people have it. I tested positive. How likely is it that I actually have it? -->

(1,000 × 0.001 × 0.99) ÷ (0.001 × 0.99 + 0.999 × 0.01) = 99 ÷ 1,098 ≈ **9%**, so you probably don't have it.

Take 100,000 people:
- **100 have the disease**, and 99 of them test positive.
- **99,900 don't have it**, and 1% of them (999) test positive anyway.
- Of the 1,098 positive results, only 99 are real, which is about 1 in 11.

The disease is rare, so false positives from the healthy majority outnumber true positives. This assumes "99% accurate" means the test catches 99% of sick people and clears 99% of healthy ones. If your test's figures differ, check its sensitivity and specificity, because the answer shifts a lot.

Your next step is a confirmatory test, which should settle it. If you have symptoms or risk factors, your real chance is higher than 9%, because the 1-in-1,000 figure applies to the general population.
