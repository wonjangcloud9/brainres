<!-- Q: Humans only use 10% of their brains, right? -->

No, that's a myth: brain imaging shows you use virtually all of your brain, and different regions are active at different times, even during sleep.

Three facts work against the 10% claim:

- **Damage:** Injury to almost any brain region causes some deficit, which wouldn't happen if 90% were unused.
- **Energy:** The brain is about 2% of body weight but uses about 20% of your energy, and evolution would not keep such an expensive organ mostly idle.
- **Imaging:** fMRI and PET scans show activity across the whole brain over a day, though not every region fires at once.

The myth probably comes from early 1900s misquotes of psychologists like William James, who said people don't reach their full *potential*. That got reworded into a claim about brain *usage*.

If you want to read more, search for "10 percent brain myth" in *Scientific American* or *Psychology Today*.
