<!-- Q: Humans only use 10% of their brains, right? -->

**No, that's a myth.** You use virtually all of your brain, just not every neuron at the same moment.

- **Brain imaging:** fMRI and PET scans show activity across nearly every region over the course of a normal day, even during sleep.
- **Injury evidence:** Damage to almost any brain area causes some deficit. With 90% idle, most injuries would be harmless.
- **Energy cost:** The brain is about 2% of body weight but uses about 20% of resting energy. Evolution wouldn't keep that much costly, unused tissue.

The myth likely grew from misquoted early-1900s claims that people rarely reach their full mental potential. If you're curious, I can explain how only a small fraction of neurons fire at once and why that's efficient.
