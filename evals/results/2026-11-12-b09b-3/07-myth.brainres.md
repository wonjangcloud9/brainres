<!-- Q: Humans only use 10% of their brains, right? -->

No, that's a myth. Humans use virtually all of their brain, just not all regions at the same moment.

Brain imaging (fMRI, PET) shows activity across the whole brain over the course of a day, even during sleep. Damage to almost any region causes some deficit, which wouldn't happen if 90% were idle. The brain is also about 2% of body weight but uses ~20% of the body's energy, and evolution would not maintain such an expensive organ if 90% of it did nothing.

The myth likely persists from a misread of early neuroscience, where researchers couldn't assign a function to many brain areas, plus self-help writers who liked the idea of untapped potential.

If you want to check this yourself, search for "fMRI whole brain activation" or read the Scientific American and Johns Hopkins explainers on the 10% myth.
