<!-- Q: A test for a disease is 99% accurate and 1 in 1,000 people have it. I tested positive. How likely is it that I actually have it? -->

**About 9%.** Even with a positive result, you most likely do not have it, because the disease is rare.

Out of 100,000 people tested:
- **Have the disease:** 100 people. The test catches 99 of them (99% sensitivity).
- **Don't have it:** 99,900 people. The test wrongly flags 999 of them (1% false positive rate).

So about 1,098 people test positive, and only 99 of them actually have the disease: 99 / 1,098 ≈ 9%.

This assumes "99% accurate" means 99% sensitivity and 99% specificity. If the test's false positive rate is different, the answer changes. Check the package insert or the lab's documentation for the exact figures.

The usual next step is a confirmatory test with a different method. A second positive from an independent test raises the probability far more than the first result did.
