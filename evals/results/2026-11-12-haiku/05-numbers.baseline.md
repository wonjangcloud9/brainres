<!-- Q: A test for a disease is 99% accurate and 1 in 1,000 people have it. I tested positive. How likely is it that I actually have it? -->

You have about a **9% chance** of actually having the disease.

Imagine 100,000 people tested:

- **Have the disease:** 100 people. The test catches 99%, so about **99** test positive.
- **Don't have it:** 99,900 people. The test wrongly flags 1%, so about **999** test positive.

That gives about 1,098 positive results, of which 99 are true positives:

99 / 1,098 ≈ **9%**

The rare condition makes the false positives dominate. This assumes "99% accurate" means 99% sensitivity and 99% specificity. If your test's error rates differ, or your risk factors make you more likely than an average person to have the disease, the number changes. A confirmatory test is the usual next step, since a second positive from an independent test would raise the probability substantially.
