<!-- Q: Humans only use 10% of their brains, right? -->

No. The 10% figure is a myth. Brain imaging (PET, fMRI) shows activity across virtually all regions of the brain over a day, and damage to almost any area produces some deficit. Evolution also doesn't favor an organ that uses about 20% of the body's energy while leaving 90% idle.

The myth probably traces back to early 20th-century misreadings of neuroscience and self-help marketing, and it persists because it sounds like an untapped reserve.

If you want to check this yourself, search for "brain imaging whole-brain activity" in a review article from a neuroscience journal, or ask a neurologist. Your own brain works the same way: most regions switch on at different times for different tasks, and there is no idle 90%.
